# Recipes and cooking

Dayspring keeps your recipes, finds new ones online, and walks you through cooking one step at a time, hands free. All of it works without an AI brain.

## Your recipes

Open **Recipes** (say "open my recipes", or "show my recipes"). Five example recipes are there to start: pancakes, chili, banana bread, rice and scrambled eggs. They're marked *example*, and you can delete them.

Add a recipe any of these ways:

- **Paste or type it.** Title on the first line, then the ingredients, then the steps. Headings like *Ingredients* and *Instructions* help, and numbered steps are understood.
- **From a web page.** Paste the page's address. Dayspring reads the recipe the site publishes (most recipe sites do) and saves its picture, the site's name and the address, so you can always go back to the original.
- **By voice.** Say "add a new recipe", give it a name, read the ingredients one at a time, say "steps", read each step, then say "done".

Each recipe can have tags and be a favourite. Search by name, tag or ingredient. Deleting asks first.

## Finding recipes online

Say "find a recipe for chili", "how do I make banana bread", or "what can I make with chicken and rice". Dayspring searches the web, reads the recipe pages it finds, and shows up to **6 cards**: a photo, the title, the site, the time, servings, rating and how many ingredients. It reads out a short summary, like which one is quickest.

Then say "the second one", "number 3", "the one from Allrecipes", "the quickest one", "save the first one" or "more options", or use the card's buttons: **Use this one**, **Save for later**, **View details**.

**View details** shows the recipe as **easy steps**: short steps grouped into *Prep*, *Cooking* and *Finish*, with the ingredients each step uses, a button for every time mentioned (press it to start that timer), and oven temperatures you can switch between °F and °C.

If none of the pages have a recipe Dayspring can read, it says so and offers to open an ordinary web search instead.

**Safety.** Dayspring only reads public web pages (never addresses on your own network), gives each site a few seconds, cleans up the text it shows, and downloads pictures itself (images only, 5 MB at most) so a saved recipe never loads anything from the internet again.

## Cooking mode

Say "let's make pancakes" (or press **Use this one** / **Start cooking**). The screen switches to big text and stays awake.

| Say | What happens |
|---|---|
| "next" / "next step" / "okay done" | The next step |
| "go back" / "previous step" | The step before |
| "repeat the step" | Says it again |
| "what step am I on?" | Where you are |
| "what are the ingredients?" | Reads them |
| "how much flour?" | Just that amount |
| "double the recipe" / "make it for 8 people" / "halve it" | Scales every amount |
| "go to step 4" | Jumps there |
| "start the timer" | Starts the timer the current step mentions (several can run at once) |
| "stop cooking" | Back to the usual screen |

Step timers get the step's name, so "Your step 3 timer is done" tells you which one finished. You can still ask for anything else while cooking; the cooking words just come first.
